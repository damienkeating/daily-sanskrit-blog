## Simple Sanskrit notes for learners — Theme: Uprising and Power

- विद्रोहः (vidrohaḥ) — rebellion, revolt; literal sense: a turning hostile against authority. [rebellion / 叛乱]
- उपद्रवः (upadravaḥ) — riot, tumult; literal sense: a disorderly running upon or toward. [riot / 骚乱]
- अभिषेकः (abhiṣekaḥ) — anointing, proclamation of a ruler; literal sense: a sprinkling upon (consecration with sacred water). [coronation / 加冕]

## English

- On November 4, 512, major riots broke out in Constantinople: enraged by Emperor Anastasius's deposition of Chalcedonian patriarchs and his Monophysite changes to the liturgy, a mob attempted to proclaim Areobindus as emperor.
- The uprising exposed how deeply religious doctrine and imperial authority were intertwined in Byzantium — altering the liturgy was tantamount to challenging the faith of the capital's populace.
- The lesson is that rulers who force doctrinal change upon an unwilling population risk igniting street revolt that threatens the very stability of the throne.

## Chinese

- 公元512年11月4日,君士坦丁堡爆发大规模暴动:皇帝阿纳斯塔修斯废黜迦克墩派牧首并试图在礼拜仪式中推行一性论修改,愤怒的民众试图拥立阿里奥宾杜斯为新皇帝。
- 这场动乱暴露了拜占庭帝国宗教教义与皇权之间根深蒂固的纠缠——更改礼拜仪式无异于挑战都城民众的信仰。
- 教训是:统治者若以强力迫使民众改变信仰,街头暴动便可能动摇王座的根基。
